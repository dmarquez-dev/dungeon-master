## The mechanism, verified and sharpened

`sed -n '91,95p' …DmCard.xaml.cs` returns — `cat -A` confirming whitespace — a closing brace, a blank
line, then `/// <summary>`. **A closing brace followed by a doc comment, literally, in that window.**
T6's diagnosis holds exactly for the `:93` gloss: true of the window, false of the line.

**The `:108` half differs in kind, and that sharpens the finding.** `sed -n '113,118p'` returns
`:113-115` then `:116` — it **does not contain `:108` at all**. That gloss was not "true of the window";
it was a feature recalled from a window that never covered the line being glossed.

> **A narrow window plus recall is not a read — the recalled feature may be true of the window and false
> of the line, or may be imported onto a line the window never showed.**

This makes the finding materially stronger than "somebody skipped a read". T6 had run two real commands
against the right file and got the load-bearing answer right both times. **The failure was not
diligence; it was that the span read and the span cited were different spans, and nothing in the
workflow forced them to be the same.** That is a mechanism that survives careful agents — which is why
eight instances came from eight of them.
