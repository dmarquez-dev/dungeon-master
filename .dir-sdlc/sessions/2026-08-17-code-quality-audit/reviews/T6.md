## Rulings on the two judgment calls

**The silent-success non-merge is CORRECT**, and the decisive evidence is in UI-004 rather than in the abstract argument. UI-004's reachable-today defect is not a mutator no-op at all — `UpdateAreaCommandValidator` rejects an empty area name and drops the whole write, a failure that *already* arrives at the view model as a non-Success `Result` and is discarded, and the view model has no `ErrorMessage` property to surface it. So CORE-001's fix (giving mutators real return values) adds a second reason for a `Result` that is still thrown away, and UI-004's fix does not stop a mutator no-opping — CORE-001 states plainly that the handlers "unconditionally return `Result<bool>.Success(true)`", making the layer's own success signal unreliable regardless of caller behaviour. Neither reaches INFRA-012, whose mechanism is EF's disconnected-graph semantics at `DungeonWriteRepository.UpdateAsync:36-44`. **What unites the three is a theme, not a fix** — exactly the case the frozen merge rule excludes. Merging would also have buried an S1 and two S2s behind unfalsifiable completion criteria. The index handles the real CORE-001→UI-004 ordering by co-locating them in Session 2, which is the right instrument.

**The tabs ruling (XCUT-012) is sound and honestly presented.** Both required quotes exist verbatim on the Microsoft page: "Use four spaces for indentation. Don't use tab characters." *and* the self-disclaimer "They're meant to be an example of common C# conventions, and not an authoritative list", alongside "You can take our conventions as-is, or modify them to suit your team's needs." The record states the counter-authority openly — its own paragraph, opening "The counter-authority is real and should be stated", quoting the pro-spaces sentence *before* stating its position, with "My position is tabs" arriving after the counter-evidence rather than before. **The census was re-run independently and matches exactly**: 109 tab-only / 62 space-only / 7 no-indent under `src/` excluding `bin`/`obj`; Tests 1 tab / 44 space; every per-project figure reconciles (35+54+11+8+1 = 109; 7+1+4+1+5+44 = 62).

**XCUT-002's hedging is genuine, and the reviewer recommends running the restore experiment.** The record says "I believe it cannot", "I believe adding the `ProjectReference` would fail to restore rather than work", and "If that is right, then every consequence downstream…" — the conditional propagates properly into the consequences rather than being a token disclaimer on an otherwise-asserted claim. The reviewer's view: run it. A single `dotnet add reference` plus `dotnet restore`, reverted immediately, settles it in about two minutes, and it expects NU1201 (project not compatible with `net10.0`), confirming T6. The payoff is disproportionate to the cost because XCUT-002 is load-bearing for `MASTER-026` and for the entire Session 6 rebuild rationale — the difference between "the cheap fix is unavailable" as belief and as fact. **A recommendation, not a defect:** the record is correctly demoted to `judgment` and honestly worded, so it conforms as written.
