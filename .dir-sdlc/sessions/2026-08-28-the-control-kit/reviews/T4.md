## The blocking finding, and the round that closed it

**B1 — the code-behind doc comment named a mechanism that does not exist.** `DmSearchBar.xaml.cs:5` read "Focus and blur are painted by the **CommonStates group** in DmSearchBar.xaml". There was no `CommonStates` group; the only other occurrences were the XAML comment at `:46` and `:48` explaining why that form was **rejected**. The statement was authored by this diff — the pre-image said the painting happened in code-behind, which was true then — so the change created a false claim pointing a future reader at the abandoned approach. Confirmed by the orchestrator before dispatch.

**Resolved in round 1 by the reviewer's option 2** (point at the XAML comment, name no mechanism), which the implementer chose over option 1 and gave a better reason for than the ranking did: option 1 corrects today's wording but leaves a *second copy* of the mechanism in a second file, so the next change to the trigger must remember a summary eight lines away — precisely the failure that produced the finding. Option 2 removes the duplication rather than correcting it.

Its second argument is the load-bearing one. The two wordings fail differently: "see the comment there for why not a visual state" is false only if that comment stops existing, which is loud and grep-able; "the CommonStates group" was false while everything around it compiled and every test passed.

**The reviewer checked the pointer rather than accepting it.** `DmSearchBar.xaml:44` opens with the literal words `WHY A TRIGGER RATHER THAN A VISUAL STATE`, and the eleven lines following give the reason in full. A pointer to a comment that did not say what the pointer claimed would have been the same defect in a new costume; it is not. `grep -rc 'CommonStates'` → 2 in the XAML, 0 in the code-behind.

**Scope of the round:** the XAML is **byte-identical** against the reviewer's own backup. The code-behind diff is exactly two hunks — the `using` removal plus the summary rewrite, and the two deleted handlers. Residue greps for `SearchBorder.Stroke`, `DungeonMaster.App.Theme`, `OnSearchFocused`, `OnSearchUnfocused` and `DmTheme.` all exit 1.

## `contradicts` finding — carried to Gate 3 with options

**`SetterTarget` cannot read a `Setter` that uses `TargetName`, and that forecloses MAUI's cross-element visual-state form repository-wide.** `src/DungeonMaster.Tests/App/Theme/TokenTypeMatchesTargetPropertyTests.cs:561-569` documents that "**Two shapes reach here**" and enumerates only the keyed-`Style` and element-attached-VSM cases. A third reaches it: a cross-element setter. `SetterTarget` (`:571-596`) walks `setter.Ancestors()` and returns the nearest non-scaffolding element, never consulting `TargetName`, so the setter composes the fictitious `Entry.Border.Stroke` and the fact fails. **Reproduced by the reviewer.**

Both halves hold: the written enumeration is incomplete, and it has **already changed shipped work** — it is why T4 shipped a `DataTrigger` rather than the visual-state form `UIKIT-013` argued for, and it will block the next kit control needing the same shape.

Ranked options as the reviewer worded them:

1. **Teach `SetterTarget` to honour `TargetName`** — resolve the target from the named element rather than the ancestor walk, and strip the type prefix from the qualified `Property`. Restores the documented MAUI form for the whole repo; scoped to one method. Evidence: `repo` (`TokenTypeMatchesTargetPropertyTests.cs:571-596`).
2. **File it as a new audit item and leave T4 as shipped** — the `DataTrigger` is correct and observed working; the guard gap is real but not this task's to close. Evidence: `judgment`.
3. **Leave it and document the limitation in the `SetterTarget` doc comment** — cheapest, but preserves a guard that silently rejects valid framework XAML. Evidence: `judgment`.

## Advisories carried to Gate 3

- **A1 — three manifest line-citation discrepancies, all confirmed.** Criterion 2 cites `DmSearchBar.xaml:35-36` for the handlers; they were at `:37-38` as T3 left the file. Criterion 4 cites `DmTheme.cs:97-98`/`:91-92`, which are the `</summary>` and declaration lines — the `Resolve<Brush>` calls naming the keys are at `:99` and `:93`. Criterion 5 cites `:32` for the literal T3 replaced; `MinimumHeightRequest` is at `:34` in the pre-image. None changed what was satisfiable, and all three were satisfied.
- **A2 — the resting `Stroke="{StaticResource DmBorderTavernBrush}"` at `:17` duplicates `DmBorderTavernStyle`**, which already sets that brush (`Controls.Tavern.xaml:47`). Keeping it local is defensible — it makes the trigger's restore target explicit rather than relying on style-vs-local precedence — but it deserves a comment saying so, or removal.
- **A3 — the token-delta ratio is honestly reported as inexact.** `DmBorderTavern` is `#4DC4862A` and `DmBorderTavernHover` is `#A6C4862A`, so alpha 166/77 = 2.16; the implementer measured 1.87, attributed the gap to sub-pixel hairline coverage, and **explicitly declined to claim an exact token match**. The right call; no criterion demands more.
- **A4 — a startup-focus trap worth carrying forward.** The search Entry holds focus *at app startup*, so a naive before/after capture shows nothing and reads as "the control needs no fix". The implementer's first run hit this and caught it only because `initial` and `focused` were byte-identical. Anyone reusing T2's probe against a focus behaviour must blur deliberately before baselining. This is the mirror of T2's DPI trap: same instrument, opposite direction, and it produces a **false negative** where the DPI bug produced a false positive.
- **A5 — reviewer worktree-restore hazard.** Reproducing the VSM failure required temporary edits, and `git checkout -- <file>` restored to HEAD, which predates the task's *uncommitted* work, clobbering the implementer's XAML. The reviewer caught it with `cmp` against a byte copy saved beforehand and restored from that. Keeping a backup before editing is what made this recoverable; worth adopting as standing practice for any reviewer that edits to reproduce.
