## The four-instead-of-two deviation — ruled satisfied

Criterion 5 says there are "exactly TWO possible results" and "Both are cached and reused." The implementer cached **four** instances, two per ribbon.

**The reviewer ruled this satisfied, not a deviation needing a round**, on two independent grounds:

1. **The operative sentence is met.** Both *results* are cached and reused, per ribbon; no `LinearGradientBrush` or `GradientStop` is allocated on any selection after first paint, and allocation is bounded per `NotebookView` instead of per tap — which is the defect `UIKIT-016` records at `docs/audit/04-app-controls-and-theme.md:659`. The "exactly TWO" clause is inherited verbatim from that audit item's *suggested direction* and counts distinct gradient **results**, not instances. The criterion's own escalation trigger is narrow — it fires only if caching would hide a key string from the guard test — and did not fire.
2. **The literal reading collides with criterion 8.** The reviewer states it would have rejected the two-instance reading **regardless of the measurement**, because satisfying criterion 5 literally breaks criterion 8.

**The mechanism was upgraded from `judgment` to `repo`-grade by the reviewer.** It read `Microsoft.Maui.Controls.dll` — the exact assembly this build links — via `PEReader`:

```
Microsoft.Maui.Controls.Brush               : Microsoft.Maui.Controls.Element
Microsoft.Maui.Controls.GradientStop        : Microsoft.Maui.Controls.Element
Microsoft.Maui.Controls.LinearGradientBrush : Microsoft.Maui.Controls.GradientBrush
```

`Brush` and `GradientStop` are `Element`s and `Element` carries a single `Parent`, so one instance worn by two ribbons is genuinely re-parented on every toggle.

**How the implementer found it, which is the part worth keeping.** Its first implementation cached two brushes in `static` fields shared by both ribbons — literally what the criterion asks for — and that build differed from pre-change by **35 of 34400 px at up to 44 per channel**, reproducibly, all at glyph edges. It did not guess: it built a probe routing through `ColorByKey` but allocating fresh per call, measured **0 px** difference, and thereby exonerated `ColorByKey` and implicated instance sharing. It also measured its own noise floor first, and named a transient that differs *from itself* across runs of one binary so a reviewer would not chase a race as a regression.
