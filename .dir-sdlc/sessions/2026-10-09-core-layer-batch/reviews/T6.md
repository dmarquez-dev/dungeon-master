## The three blocking findings, and their resolution

All three were the same defect class: **a universal or headline claim whose named command measures a
narrower population than the claim.** Criterion 19's exact failure mode.

### B1 -- `CORE-033`'s universal was false for two of its six citations

It read "All six `locations:` citations in the YAML block are stale at `HEAD`". **Two were correct**:
`PlacedObject.cs:10` and `PlacedObject.cs:53`. The block printed the contradicting grep four paragraphs
earlier and spent a whole correction arguing `:53` is now right. No command sat beside the sentence.

**Resolved** by withdrawing the sentence, quoting it verbatim, and enumerating: stale `Area.cs:12`->`:27`,
`Dungeon.cs:10`->`:16`, `Floor.cs:13`->`:20`, `Area.cs:45`->`:60`; correct at `HEAD`
`PlacedObject.cs:10` and `:53`, the latter marked **correct by coincidence** so a later reader cannot
conclude the row was always right.

### B2 -- `CORE-026`'s universal was false for one of four ranges, and its stated reason could not reach it

It read "All four `locations:` ranges in the YAML block are stale for the same reason" -- that expansion
displaced everything below. **`CanvasPoint.cs:3-8` did not move**: identical at `00a093a` and at `HEAD`,
with the file's three expanded tags at `:34`, `:35`, `:84` all **below** it.

**Resolved** by a four-way enumeration naming each boundary by line **and** content. The implementer
**departed from the dispatch's wording deliberately** on one item and was right to: `PlacedObjectDto.cs:6-15`
is **stale, not displaced**, because its start boundary did not move and its length grew. The reviewer
agreed -- "a displaced range is a right-length span at a wrong position; this span's start did not move and
its length grew, so calling it displaced would assert an offset that does not exist" -- which is the exact
distinction B2 exists to preserve.

### B3 -- the `MASTER-130` cell claimed `obj` cleared on a one-file command

Its headline said two of three abbreviations were cleared, evidenced only by `grep -nw 'obj'` on
`FloorObjectMethods.cs`. The row's own title says "throughout **both layers**". **T5's review had handed
this over explicitly**, naming `DungeonReadRepository.cs:78` as a real violation of the same rule and
censusing the wider population. The cell reported `ct`'s residue in full and `obj`'s not at all.

**Resolved** by withdrawing the headline with its single-file cause stated, replacing it per-abbreviation,
and adding `EXCLUSION 3` mirroring `EXCLUSION 2`. Status stays `part done`; **no verdict changed**.

**The figures in that finding were wrong, and the implementer corrected them.** The dispatch and the
reviewer's round-1 report both said `PlacedObjectConfiguration.cs` holds 10 `obj` lines and that 18 are
non-exempt. Measured: **9 lines and 18 occurrences** in that file, so **17 non-exempt lines across 3
files**, all in Infrastructure. Per-file 9 / 6 / 2 / 4, total 21. The orchestrator confirmed, and the
reviewer acknowledged the error originated in its own round-1 report and was inherited by the dispatch.
**A figure carried between parties without recomputation, inside a finding about exactly that.**

## Criterion 18 -- a self-caught violation

The implementer's first drafts wrote two commit hashes **30 times** across both files. The criterion
forbids any. It removed them all and offered byte-identical `grep -oE` inventories against the pre-edit
copies as proof. **The reviewer verified independently and found the claim exact** -- 90 distinct tokens
for the index, 15 for the domain file, every count equal.

It also found an **eighth instrument trap** in the process: `\b[0-9a-f]{7,40}\b` returns 0 and is **blind**,
because word boundaries cannot bound a hex run inside a longer hex run, so it silently misses every
sha256. Re-run lower-bounded as `[0-9a-f]{7,}`, the added lines yield two labelled file-content hashes,
the migration timestamp `20260818232300`, and `cceeded` -- the interior of "succeeded".
