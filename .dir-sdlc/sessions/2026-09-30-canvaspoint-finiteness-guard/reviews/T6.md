## Ruling on criterion 5, which T6 flagged against itself

Criterion 5 says the doc comment **must not say** that SQLite rejects non-finite floats. T6's comment
contains that exact sentence — **as a quotation it then rejects.** T6 raised this itself rather than hoping
it passed.

**Ruling: satisfied.** In the reviewer's terms:

> The forbidden proposition appears only at `:40-41` as a quotation the sentence then rejects — *"would be
> wrong in both directions and is not what these tests measure"* — immediately after the file positively
> asserts the true statement. **"Must not say X" governs assertion, not the occurrence of the token string;
> a proposition quoted and denied is not said, it is refuted**, and a reader comes away believing the
> opposite more firmly than if the sentence were merely absent. Reading the letter as banning the string
> would forbid warning a reader off the misconception at all — the inverse of the clause's stated purpose
> (*"which is false for two of the three values"*).

**The attribution's contrastive half was measured, not argued.** Forcing `Position_X = NULL` yields
`SqliteException : SQLite Error 19: 'NOT NULL constraint failed'` — a constraint code, **categorically unlike
the NaN case's unwrapped `InvalidOperationException`.** That is what makes "not a SQLite constraint" a
measured claim.
